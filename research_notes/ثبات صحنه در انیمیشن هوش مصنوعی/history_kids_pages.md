# Running AI-animated Persian history and Persian kids pages (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: research ran on 2026-11-22. WebFetch was blocked in this session (support.google.com, techcrunch.com, fortune.com failed DNS). So every finding below comes from search-result snippets and summaries, not full page reads. The official policy pages are cited by URL, but their exact current wording was not checked directly. ## 1. Successful AI history pages: formats, hooks, accuracy

### Takeaway
The proven AI-history formats are (a) "POV / time-traveller vlog" shorts (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), (b) long "boring history for sleep" narrations on YouTube, and (c) maps and explainers. All three work, but the field is crowded and heavily criticised for errors and anachronisms. YouTube's 2025–2026 inauthentic-content rules target the mass-produced end of it. The way to stand out is a human-researched script with sources, a consistent character or host, and a real story arc.

### Cited Findings
- AI "time-traveller" vlogs, where a fake vlogger "visits" a historical era, became a viral trend. Named creators: Dan (POV Lab), Hogne (Time Traveller POV) and Chloe VS History. The Guardian covered the trend ("We can stitch together our past", Priya Bharadia). — [inkl mirror of the Guardian piece](https://www.inkl.com/news/we-can-stitch-together-our-past-the-ai-generated-time-travellers-vlogging-from-history); [aitopics](https://aitopics.org/doc/news:05C09D52)
- Chloe VS History has more than 100k TikTok followers. The June 2026 profile is framed around the debate over AI history influencers. — [Hola US, Jun 2026](https://www.hola.com/us/lifestyle/20260626909651/ai-history-influencer-tiktok-debate/)
- Historians questioned these videos' accuracy, citing AI anachronisms such as sunglasses in ancient Rome. Creators reply that the videos aim to evoke a period's feel and do not replace education. Oxford's Adam Smith is cited as saying the format can complement classroom teaching. — [getcoai](https://getcoai.com/news/ai-generated-history-videos-are-going-viral-on-tiktok-but-how-accurate-are-they); [Ground News summary](https://ground.news/article/are-the-viral-videos-of-ai-pov-spreading-an-inaccurate-story); [Travel Tomorrow](https://traveltomorrow.com/reimagining-history-tiktoks-ai-time-travel-sparks-debate/)
- The "POV: you wake up on [historical day]" format is analysed as a genre. — [Dettmann Substack](https://dettmann.substack.com/p/pov-you-wake-up-on-historical-day)
- Belgian broadcaster VRT covered AI history videos in February 2025, so the trend predates 2026. — [VRT NWS, 2025-02-27](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/02/27/geschiedenis-video-s-gemaakt-met-ai/)
- "Boring history" sleep channels, reported by 404 Media around September 2025:
  - The channels include Sleepless Historian, Boring History Bites and Dreamoria.
  - Sleepless Historian had about 614k subscribers, posted a multi-hour video daily, and had 3 videos above 1M views.
  - Historians said the videos contain inaccurate facts and visuals and dominate search for topics like the American Revolution.
  - Pete Kelly (History Time): "it used to take six months to make a video, now someone can do the same thing in a day."
  - Sources: [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/ai-generated-boring-history-videos-are-flooding-youtube-and-drowning-out-real-history/); [Der Standard](https://www.derstandard.at/story/3000000287806/kigeschichtskanaele-verdraengen-echte-historiker-auf-youtube)
- NPR (Aug 2025): AI slop videos rack up views and ad money on YouTube and TikTok. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/08/28/nx-s1-5493485/ai-slop-videos-youtube-tiktok)
- A creator guide warns that AI writing tools generate plausible historical claims with invented dates, casualty figures and attributed quotes. It also says the history niche is crowded, with incumbent channels in the millions of subscribers. This is a vendor guide, so treat it as low-authority. — [FluxNote guide](https://fluxnote.io/guides/history-facts-shorts-guide)
- A vendor blog lists historical POV among the dominant AI video formats of 2026. This is commercial and low-authority. — [OpenArt blog](https://openart.ai/blog/ai-youtube-channel-ideas/)

### Inferences
- **Formats for a Persian history page:**
  - Short: POV or time-traveller vlogs, e.g. "a day in Persepolis under Darius", "a merchant on the Silk Road in Nishapur".
  - Medium (3–8 min): explainers with animated maps, e.g. "how the Safavid borders changed", or "talking historical figure" monologues framed clearly as dramatisation.
  - Long: calm narrated "history to fall asleep to" in Persian. This is a likely under-served niche, since no evidence of Persian competitors was found.
- **Accuracy routine:**
  - Write the script from named sources (e.g. Encyclopaedia Iranica, Cambridge History of Iran) and put a source line in the caption.
  - Keep a list of anachronisms to check per era: clothing, weapons, writing script (e.g. no Nastaliq calligraphy in the Achaemenid era), architecture, foods (no tomatoes or potatoes before the Columbian exchange).
  - Mark invented dialogue as dramatisation.
- Differentiation matters under YouTube's "generic or repetitive" category (Section 3). Use a recurring host character, original analysis or commentary, and a varied structure. Do not reuse the same template across hundreds of uploads.

### Gaps
- No verified analytics (views, retention, length benchmarks) for specific AI history channels. The only numbers are 404 Media's subscriber counts.
- No sources found on successful Persian-language AI history pages specifically.
- No data-backed optimal lengths or hooks. Platform norms (Shorts up to 3 min, Reels about 15–90 s) are general knowledge and were not verified here.

## 2. Kids content: what works, age bands, pacing, quality principles, Cocomelon criticism

### Takeaway
Children's media is the most scrutinised AI category of 2026. Exposés by the NYT and others, a letter from 200+ advocacy groups, and YouTube limiting AI content on YouTube Kids to "a small set of high-quality channels" all point the same way. A Persian kids page should be slow-paced, educational in substance, non-commercial and human-reviewed, aimed at a clear age band. It should avoid the repetitive, bizarre or "deceptively educational" patterns that YouTube demonetises.

### Cited Findings
- **The AI "slop for kids" problem:**
  - A NYT investigation (early 2026) found many AI channels targeting toddlers and preschoolers that market themselves as "educational", some of them on YouTube Kids. YouTube removed the hyper-realistic examples the Times shared, one for violating child-safety policy. — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/social-media/investigation-finds-youtube-is-serving-mindless-ai-slop-to-toddlers-and-preschoolers); [The Star / NYT syndication, 2026-03-11](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/03/11/how-ai-generated-videos-are-distorting-your-childs-youtube-feed)
  - After the NYT investigation, YouTube suspended 5 channels from the Partner Program. — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/social-media/investigation-finds-youtube-is-serving-mindless-ai-slop-to-toddlers-and-preschoolers)
  - Over 200 organizations sent a letter (about 2026-04-01) asking YouTube to ban AI-generated content from YouTube Kids and label it across the platform. YouTube said it limits AI content on YouTube Kids to a small set of high-quality channels and is developing labels for YouTube Kids. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/01/ai-slop-200-organizations-letter-youtube-google/); [AOL/HuffPost](https://www.aol.com/finance/more-200-child-advocacy-groups-023209076.html)
  - Fairplay estimated that top AI slop channels targeting children earn more than $4.25M a year. A Kapwing analysis found 21% of sampled feed videos were AI-generated. Both figures come via secondary coverage. — [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/youtube-ai-slop-for-children); [Undark, 2026-03-20](https://undark.org/2026/03/20/ai-slop-children/)
  - An academic study, "Slop for Kids" (AoIR 2025), found AI children's videos are mostly music and stories, plus educational and religious content. Most did not get high view counts, with a few exceptions. — [SPIR/AoIR](https://spir.aoir.org/ojs/index.php/spir/article/view/15158)
  - Reports describe AI kids videos that model unsafe or toxic behaviour. — [GadgetReview](https://www.gadgetreview.com/?p=366366); [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/ai-slop-is-flooding-childrens-media-parents-should-be-very-alarmed/)
  - YouTube CEO Neal Mohan named managing AI slop and improving kids and teen content as 2026 priorities. — [Digit](https://www.digit.in/features/general/youtube-ceo-reducing-ai-slop-videos-enhancing-kids-and-teen-content-key-focus-in-2026.html)
- **YouTube's quality principles for kids content**, written with child-development experts:
  - High-quality made-for-kids content is promoted in recommendations. Low-quality content can get limited or no ads.
  - Named low-quality traits: heavily commercial or promotional content (e.g. unboxing that encourages excessive buying), content encouraging negative behaviours or attitudes, and "deceptively educational" content.
  - YouTube says views of principle-compliant content in YouTube Kids rose by more than 45%.
  - Sources: [YouTube Help: Best practices for kids & family content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en); [PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/helpcenter/best_practices_for_kids_and_family_content_en.pdf); [quality principles PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/hytw_yt_quality_content_principles.pdf); [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/enabling-high-quality-youtube-kids-experience/)
- **Pacing research:**
  - Lillard & Peterson (2011, *Pediatrics*): 60 four-year-olds watched 9 minutes of a fast-paced cartoon (SpongeBob), a slow-paced educational one (Caillou), or drew. The fast-paced group did worse on executive-function tasks right afterwards. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2011/09/12/140401099/spongebob-may-be-too-speedy-for-preschool-brains); [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna44483903)
  - Later work is mixed. Some studies point to fantastical content, not pace, as the driver, and effects were short-term only. — [BMC Psychology 2024 systematic review](https://bmcpsychology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40359-024-01738-1/tables/2); [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/24124/3/P41%20uploaded%20version.pdf); [Lillard lab PDF](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/early-development-lab/files/the_immediate_impact_8.pdf)
- **Cocomelon criticism:**
  - An AAP Q&A notes that experts criticise CoComelon for a too-fast pace, features that "capture a child's attention but don't let it go", and shallow educational content. — [AAP Q&A portal](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/watching-cocomelon-and-guidance-on-early-childhood-development/)
  - One source claims scenes change every 1–3 seconds with zooms and pans. This is a secondary blog, unverified. — [Green Matters](https://greenmatters.com/parenting/why-is-cocomelon-bad-for-kids)
  - Researchers note there is no empirical study of CoComelon specifically. — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/cocomelon-cause-tantrums-speech-delays-175331866.html)
  - Moonbug (CoComelon's studio) partnered with UCLA's Center for Scholars and Storytellers and pushes back against "addictive" claims (July 2026). — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2612271/cocomelon-creators-push-back-against-addictive-claims-with-new-child-development-initiative)
  - Common Sense Media has a parents' guide. — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/tv-reviews/cocomelon)
- **AAP screen guidance (2016 policy, still the commonly cited one):**
  - Under 18 months: no media except video chat.
  - 18–24 months: only high-quality programming, watched with a caregiver.
  - 2–5 years: at most 1 hour a day of high-quality programs, ideally co-viewed.
  - Screens should not displace sleep or play, and should be off at meals and 1 hour before bed.
  - Sources: [NPR 2016](https://www.npr.org/sections/ed/2016/10/21/498550475/american-academy-of-pediatrics-lifts-no-screens-under-2-rule); [Zero to Three](https://www.zerotothree.org/resources/screen-time); [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5836000/table/children-05-00031-t001)

### Inferences
- **Age bands:**
  - 2–5: songs and nursery rhymes (Persian "laalaayi" lullabies, counting, colours, the alphabet), 2–4 min.
  - 4–7: short stories with a moral, e.g. from Kelileh-o-Demneh or folk tales like "Khaleh Sooskeh" and "Kadoo Ghelgheleh Zan", 3–7 min.
  - 6–10: educational explainers (animals, science, Nowruz traditions), 4–8 min.
- **Production guidance (from the pacing literature and the Cocomelon critique):**
  - Slower cuts, with shots held for several seconds rather than 1–3 s.
  - Fewer zooms and whip-pans, a calm palette rather than constantly saturated colour, and clear speech at a slow tempo.
  - Repetition for learning, but pause for the child to "answer".
  - A coherent narrative, realistic-enough characters, no bizarre AI morphing.
- **AI-specific risks:** extra fingers, morphing faces and object glitches make content "off-putting" and erode parents' trust. Every frame needs human review before upload.
- Reaching YouTube Kids with AI-made content is now gated to "a small set of high-quality channels". A new AI kids page should expect to grow on main YouTube (made-for-kids) and Instagram first.

### Gaps
- The official text of YouTube Kids eligibility criteria for AI content (what "small set" means, and how a channel qualifies) was not found.
- No newer AAP statement on AI-generated content for children was found.
- No data on Persian-language kids channels (Iranian or diaspora) and their formats.

## 3. Platform rules: made for kids/COPPA, YouTube inauthentic content, AI labels, Google GenAI policy

### Takeaway
- **YouTube** demonetises at channel level any content that is "generic or repetitive", "unsatisfying or off-putting", or "AI personas on sensitive topics". The policy was renamed in July 2025 and clarified in July 2026.
- **Disclosure:** YouTube requires a label for realistic synthetic content, but fully animated or clearly unrealistic content is exempt.
- **Made for kids:** a COPPA designation, mandatory if the content targets under-13s. It disables personalised ads, comments and other features. Mislabelling has cost Disney $10M (2025).
- **Meta and TikTok** auto-label content from C2PA/IPTC metadata and require labels on photorealistic video and realistic audio.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube inauthentic content (YPP):**
  - On 2025-07-15 "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content", clarified to cover mass-produced and repetitive content.
  - It applies to the channel as a whole, so a few violating videos can demonetise the whole channel.
  - Using AI tools is not by itself disqualifying.
  - Sources: [YouTube YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos)
- **July 2026 clarification**, three non-monetisable categories:
  1. Generic or repetitive content: low educational value, little commentary, near-identical videos, the same characters in the same situation over and over.
  2. Unsatisfying or off-putting content: disturbing themes like violence or loss repeated without a cohesive narrative, animal-peril loops, AI clips stitched together without narrative logic.
  3. AI personas on sensitive topics: an AI presented as a human expert advising on health, legal, finance or politics.
  - Tubefilter calls it a clarification, not a new policy. TechTimes calls the standard more subjective.
  - Sources: [Tubefilter 2026-07-13](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/); [TechCrunch 2026-07-20](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/youtube-cracks-down-on-off-putting-content-and-ai-slop-2000787956); [TechTimes 2026-07-15](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320629/20260715/youtube-wiped-35m-subscribers-over-ai-slop-now-its-judging-your-taste.htm); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2226445/youtube-explains-ai-slop-policy-why-some-creators-wont-get-paid/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-ai-content-rules-monetization/)
- **Enforcement:** 16 channels with a combined 35M subscribers were terminated in January 2026, per TheNextWeb (2026-06-15). — [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage?type=AI); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320629/20260715/youtube-wiped-35m-subscribers-over-ai-slop-now-its-judging-your-taste.htm)
- **YouTube altered or synthetic content disclosure** (launched March 2024):
  - Creators must disclose meaningfully altered or synthetic content that seems realistic.
  - Unrealistic or animated content and minor edits are exempt. YouTube's own examples include "someone riding a unicorn through a fantastical world" and an animated missile in a fully animated video.
  - YouTube may add a label itself if content could mislead.
  - Disclosing does not limit reach or monetisation.
  - Sources: [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491); [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content); ["How this content was made"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15447836?hl=en)
- **Kids gap in disclosure:** because animation is exempt, most AI kids content carries no label. — [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/youtube-ai-slop-for-children)
- **COPPA / made for kids:**
  - 2019 FTC and New York AG settlement with YouTube/Google ($170M). YouTube had used persistent identifiers on child-directed channels for targeted ads. — [FTC blog 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/youtube-channel-owners-your-content-directed-children)
  - Sept 2025 FTC settlement with Disney ($10M). Disney had mislabelled child-directed videos as "not made for kids", which enabled data collection, targeted ads, autoplay and public comments for kids. — [FTC blog, Sept 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/09/protecting-children-watching-youtube-videos-lessons-learned-ftcs-settlement-disney)
  - If a channel is set to "not made for kids", every video defaults to that setting. Any video intended for under-13s must be set "made for kids" individually. — [same FTC source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/09/protecting-children-watching-youtube-videos-lessons-learned-ftcs-settlement-disney)
- **Meta / Instagram:**
  - The "Made with AI" label was renamed "AI info". For content only edited with AI, the label moves to the post menu.
  - Meta detects C2PA and IPTC "AI generated" metadata.
  - Meta may require an AI label on photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio, and may penalise unlabelled content.
  - Sources: [Meta Help: identify AI content](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/); [Meta Transparency: Labeling AI content](https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content); [Meta newsroom Feb 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/)
  - Instagram is testing a voluntary profile-level "AI creator" label (May 2026). — [Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2026/5/instagram-tests-voluntary-ai-creator-label-on-profiles)
- **TikTok:** realistic synthetic people, events or voices need a visible disclosure, and TikTok auto-labels on detecting C2PA metadata. This comes from a marketing guide only and was not verified against TikTok's documentation. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure)
- **Google Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy:** bans child sexual abuse or exploitation content and attempts to generate it. Enforcement is automated plus human review, and repeat violations can restrict the account. The version found was last modified 2024-12-17. — [Google GenAI Prohibited Use Policy](https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy); [Gemini help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16625148)

### Inferences
- **History page:**
  - Mark realistic AI reenactments as altered/synthetic on YouTube, which costs nothing.
  - Avoid "AI historian giving political advice" framing, which falls under category 3 for political topics. This matters for Iranian modern history and politics.
- **Kids page:**
  - Set "made for kids" at channel level. Expect lower ad income (no personalised ads) and no comments or notifications.
  - Avoid the repetitive same-characters, same-situation formula that the July 2026 examples describe almost exactly.
- **Instagram:** generated files from Veo or Sora-type tools may carry C2PA metadata and get "AI info" auto-labelled. Code-rendered animation like this repo's Remotion pipeline likely carries none, but voluntary disclosure builds trust. This is an inference, not verified.
- **Cross-platform:** a human-voiced or human-scripted element plus an original narrative is the strongest defence on every platform.

### Gaps
- The official July 2026 YouTube policy text, the TikTok AIGC policy page, and Veo/Gemini minimum ages and child-content rules were all not retrieved directly.
- Whether YouTube Kids now requires AI labelling was not confirmed. YouTube only said labels are "in development".

## 4. Ethics and legal: historical figures, religious figures (Iran), copyrighted characters

### Takeaway
In Iranian and Shia contexts, depicting the faces of the Prophet, the Imams and their families is the main red line. Even respectful films have been pulled. Animated female characters face hijab expectations for Iranian-produced work. For historical figures generally, label dramatisations and avoid fabricated quotes. No research was gathered on copyrighted characters; the default is to use only original characters.

### Cited Findings
- **Iran's film code (1983)** bans direct or indirect insult to the Prophets, the Imams and the Supreme Leader. — [La Vie des idées PDF, 2026-05-19](https://laviedesidees.fr/IMG/pdf/20260519_irancine.pdf)
- **Shia theology** is described as forbidding reproduction of the faces of the Imams and their families, though such images are widely available and popular. — [Quartz](https://qz.com/568430/irans-whimsical-censorship-system-made-one-religious-film-a-blockbuster-while-banning-another); [Al-Monitor 2015](https://al-monitor.com/originals/2015/08/iran-rastakhiz-ban.html)
- **The *Rastakhiz* case:** this 2014 film about Abbas, Imam Hussein's half-brother, was pulled on its first day after clerics objected to how Abbas was depicted, despite 40 minutes of cuts. — [Iran Human Rights](https://iranhumanrights.org/?p=20509); [Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2015/08/20/despite-president-rouhanis-promises-films-continue-to-be-banned-in-iran/)
- **Hijab in animation:** Khamenei ruled that women in Iranian-made cartoons and animation must wear hijab. Foreign films may be screened, but local productions may not show unscarved women. — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/irans-supreme-leader-says-women-in-cartoons-must-wear-hijabs/)
- **Approvals:** Iranian filmmakers need three authorisations (script, filming, release). This applies to formal cinema, not necessarily social media pages. — [Quartz](https://qz.com/568430/irans-whimsical-censorship-system-made-one-religious-film-a-blockbuster-while-banning-another)
- **Fabricated quotes:** a creator guide warns that AI fabricates quotes attributed to historical figures. — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/history-facts-shorts-guide)

### Inferences
- **Religious figures:**
  - Never show the faces of the Prophet Muhammad, the Imams or Ahl al-Bayt. Use light, silhouette, calligraphy or an off-screen narrator, as traditional Iranian religious painting does with a veiled face or halo.
  - This also protects reach with religious audiences and avoids legal risk for creators or audiences inside Iran.
- **Historical rulers (Cyrus, Darius, Shah Abbas, Nader Shah):**
  - These are acceptable to depict. Base the look on coins, reliefs or portraits, and caption invented dialogue as "dramatised".
  - Avoid making modern political figures speak through AI. That is a deepfake risk, and political topics are sensitive on YouTube (category 3) and in Iran.
- **Kids page:** for creators or audiences inside Iran, female characters in modest dress or hijab avoid friction. For a diaspora-only audience this is a choice, not a requirement. Inference.
- **Copyrighted characters:** do not generate Peppa, Bluey, Disney or Shrek lookalikes. Use original or public-domain folk characters (Mollah Nasreddin, Khaleh Sooskeh, Kelileh-o-Demneh animals). This is general IP principle, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No primary source on the formal legal status in Iran of depicting the Prophet on social media versus cinema.
- No sources were gathered on copyright and trademark risk for AI lookalike characters, or on platform rules on depicting real deceased people.
- Any Iranian law specific to AI or online video (e.g. registration rules for Instagram businesses) was not researched.

## 5. Persian-language specifics: TTS, subtitles, audience behaviour

### Takeaway
- **Voices:** ElevenLabs (multilingual models) is the likely highest-quality Persian TTS, though no independent benchmark exists. Azure (2 Persian neural voices) and Google (Standard/WaveNet) are cheaper fallbacks.
- **Audience:** Persian audiences rely heavily on VPNs and Instagram.
- **Monetisation:** YouTube now detects VPN users and classes much Persian traffic as in-Iran, where sanctions mean no ad revenue. This crushes ad income for creators with audiences inside Iran.

### Cited Findings
- **ElevenLabs** markets Persian TTS and STT. Its own page claims the best quality, which is marketing. It also advertises "Eleven v4" as its most expressive model. — [ElevenLabs Persian TTS](https://elevenlabs.io/text-to-speech/persian); [Chinese-language page mentioning v4](https://elevenlabs.io/zh/text-to-speech/persian)
- **Developer comparison:** a third-party table rates ElevenLabs' Persian support as "growing" with the highest naturalness. It rates Azure as steady with SSML support and a free F0 tier. — [VideoSDK](https://www.videosdk.live/developer-hub/tts/persian-text-to-speech)
- **Azure:** 2 Persian neural voices (fa-IR), per an aggregator. — [json2video](https://json2video.com/ai-voices/azure/languages/persian/)
- **Google Cloud TTS:** lists Persian Standard and WaveNet tiers. — [Google Cloud voices](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/voices)
- **Research baseline:** Persian TTS research systems score MOS about 3.7–4.1. Common Voice Persian is too low quality for TTS training (ParsVoice paper, Oct 2025). — [arXiv 2510.10774](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10774v2)
- **Instagram reach:** 50 Persian Instagram accounts have more than 3M followers and 600+ have more than 1M (older Atlantic Council figure). Instagram is the main home for Persian user-generated creators outside state and diaspora media. — [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/in-depth-research-reports/report/iranians-on-socialmedia/); [Filterwatch](https://medium.com/filterwatch/https-medium-com-filterwatch-nofilter-how-iran-deals-with-its-instagram-influencers-b20897b97ccd)
- **VPN use:** Iran is a top VPN market, and nearly every smartphone user has anti-filter software (2022). — [Iran International 2022](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202208204278); [Stimson](https://stimson.org/2023/internet-filtering-in-iran-boosts-vpn-business-much-of-it-government-owned/)
- **YouTube income:** better VPN and location detection on YouTube classifies large parts of Persian audiences as inside Iran, and creator income has dropped dramatically, sometimes overnight. — [NIAC Insights](https://insights.niacouncil.org/p/when-sanctions-silence-iranian-voices)

### Inferences
- **Voice:**
  - Test ElevenLabs first for narration. Generate a short A/B in each voice with the same sentences, including ezafe, numbers and Arabic loanwords.
  - Build a pronunciation dictionary for names (Xšayāršā, Khwarazm, Ghaznavid).
  - For kids, a warm, slow voice. A real human voice (recorded by the creator) may be the most trusted option and strengthens "authentic" signals on YouTube.
- **Subtitles:** burn in Persian subtitles (RTL, a clear font like Vazirmatn), because most viewers watch muted. Add English subtitles on the history page to reach second-generation diaspora and non-Persian viewers.
- **Monetisation plan:**
  - Do not depend on YouTube AdSense if most of the audience is inside Iran.
  - Prioritise diaspora audiences (Canada, USA, Germany, UK) for ad revenue.
  - Use Instagram for reach.
  - Diversify via sponsorships, memberships, or selling story and song packs to diaspora parents.
